# The Cumberland River and Nashville

The Cumberland River is a major waterway of the southeastern United States. It flows 688 miles from its headwaters in the Appalachian foothills of eastern Kentucky, through northern Tennessee, and back into western Kentucky before joining the Ohio River. The river drains a basin of about 18,000 square miles. The USGS gage number 03438220 near Grand Rivers, Kentucky, records the river's flow.

Native American peoples including the Cherokee, Chickasaw, and Shawnee used the Cumberland for trade, travel, and settlement long before European contact. The river's name comes from the 1750 expedition of Dr. Thomas Walker, who named it for the Duke of Cumberland. French traders had earlier called it the Shawnee River.

Nashville's founding in 1779 by James Robertson and John Donelson was tied directly to the river. Donelson led a flotilla of flatboats carrying families up the Cumberland to the site of Fort Nashborough in the winter of 1779 and 1780. The settlement grew on the bluff above the river because the river was the only road.

Steamboats reached Nashville in 1819, and the river became the city's link to the cotton and tobacco markets of New Orleans. By the 1850s Nashville was a major river port, and warehouses lined the waterfront. Cotton, tobacco, iron, and lumber moved downriver; manufactured goods came back up.

The river also brought danger. The flood of 1927 covered much of the low city. The flood of May 2010 crested at 51.86 feet, put the Opryland area and the downtown riverfront under water, and caused more than two billion dollars in damage in Nashville alone.

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built a system of locks and dams on the Cumberland beginning in the 1920s. Wolf Creek Dam, completed in 1951, created Lake Cumberland. Old Hickory Dam, completed in 1954, created Old Hickory Lake just upstream of Nashville, and Cheatham Dam below the city regulates the river's level through downtown.

Today the Port of Nashville handles barge traffic in aggregates, grain, and steel. The riverfront has been rebuilt as parkland. Cumberland Park, the pedestrian bridge, and the Ascend Amphitheater sit on the banks where the warehouses stood. The river remains the reason the city is where it is.
