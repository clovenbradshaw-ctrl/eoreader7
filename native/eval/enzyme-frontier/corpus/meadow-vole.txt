The meadow vole (Microtus pennsylvanicus) is a small rodent of the
northeastern grasslands. Adults weigh between 33 and 65 grams, and the
body measures 8 to 13 centimeters without the tail.

Voles eat grasses, sedges, and the bark of young saplings in winter.
They cut runways through the thatch — narrow clipped paths, an inch
wide — and line their nests with shredded grass. A single meadow may
hold forty voles to the acre in a peak year.

Breeding runs from March through October. A female bears litters of
four to six pups, and the pups open their eyes on the eighth day. Owls,
hawks, foxes, and weasels hunt the vole; the short-eared owl takes more
voles than any other predator in these fields.

The vole does not hibernate. Under the snow it keeps its runways open,
feeding on cached stems through the cold months. Spring melt floods the
low runways, and the colony rebuilds on higher ground.
