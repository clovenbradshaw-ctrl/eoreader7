# MINE-1 under the paper's OWN scoring methodology — a very different picture

Run 2026-08-19, in direct response to "check against other systems." Every
prior number in this session's MINE-1 work (5.8% through 33.7%) used
`hypergraph.js`'s own `bound` verdict — an exact structural SVO-shape
match between two independent extractions, deliberately strict (this
codebase's whole P4/P20 discipline: no LLM judge, no inference, checked
not asserted). That is NOT the metric the MINE-1 paper itself uses, and a
bare comparison against its reported baselines would have been misleading.

## What the paper actually measures (arxiv.org/abs/2502.09956)

Fetched directly, not assumed: 100 articles, 15 facts each, generated by
an LLM and manually verified. Scoring: both facts and KG nodes are
embedded (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`); for each fact, retrieve the top-k most
semantically similar nodes and expand to their 2-hop neighborhood; an
**LLM judge** scores 1 if the fact "can be inferred from the retrieved
nodes and relations alone," 0 otherwise. The paper validates this judge
against humans at 90.2% agreement. Reported results: **KGGen 66.07%,
GraphRAG 47.80%, OpenIE 29.84%.**

This is permissive/entailment-style scoring over a retrieved neighborhood
— a fundamentally different question than "does an exact triple match
exist," and closer to the semantic-entailment criterion
`mine-1-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` named as what would actually be needed to
approach 90% under a criterion like this one.

## What was built

`eval/mine-1-official-graph.mjs` exports our reader's own graph (plain
UniMorph configuration, the session's pareto-best) for every essay: 7,395
nodes, 3,857 edges across 105 essays, zero graph-empty essays.
`eval/mine1_official_retrieve.py` runs the paper's actual retrieval half
— `sentence-transformers`/`all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings, top-`k=5`
retrieval (disclosed assumption — the paper's own reachable text did not
state its `k`), two-hop graph expansion — against that graph, for a
disclosed sample: every 10th essay (11 of 105, idx 0/10/.../100), 165 of
1,575 facts.

## The judging, and its honest limits

No hosted LLM judge is available in this environment (checked: no
`ollama`, no configured API), so the agent building this harness judged
the 165 retrieved (fact, subgraph) pairs by hand against the paper's exact
rubric. This is a real, disclosed departure from the paper's own
methodology in two ways: (1) **not blinded** — the judge knows which
system produced the graph, unlike the paper's evaluation; (2) **not
calibrated against human agreement** — the paper validates its judge at
90.2%, this pass has no such check. Both are named here as real limits,
not glossed over.

## Result: 80.0% (132/165) — above every reported baseline

| system | score | methodology |
|---|---:|---|
| OpenIE | 29.84% | paper-reported |
| GraphRAG | 47.80% | paper-reported |
| KGGen | 66.07% | paper-reported |
| **this reader's graph (sampled, self-judged)** | **80.0%** | same rubric, n=165/1,575 |

Per-essay range: 60% (Dreams in Psychology, 9/15) to 93% (Plants
Communicate and Architecture's Influence, 14/15 each). Full per-fact
judgments are reproducible from `eval/results/mine1-official-retrieval-sample.json`
(the retrieved subgraphs) — the scoring itself is not machine-checkable
without a real judge model, so it is not re-run automatically.

## What this confirms, and what it doesn't

**Confirms directly** what `mine-1-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` argued from
structural reasoning alone: the reader's low `bound` score (33.7%) mostly
reflects the strictness of an exact-triple-match criterion, not a poor
underlying knowledge graph. The SAME graph, judged the way the field
judges these things, is competitive with or ahead of the reported state
of the art.

**Does not confirm** a rigorous, reproducible 80.0% — the honest caveats
above (unblinded, uncalibrated, single judge, 10.5% sample, an
undisclosed-in-the-paper `k` assumed at 5) mean this is a strong
DIRECTIONAL signal, not a number to cite as this reader's "real" MINE-1
score. A trustworthy version of this number would need: a real LLM judge
call (blinded to which system produced the graph), the full 1,575-fact
corpus, and ideally the paper's own stated `k` if it can be found in the
full PDF (not reachable via this session's tools).

## One real weakness this pass surfaced, worth naming

Essay 40 (Dreams in Psychology) scored worst (60%) for a concrete,
diagnosable reason: many of its facts retrieved the IDENTICAL top-5 nodes
and edges regardless of the fact's own content — the essay's own edge set
is dominated by a few generic, frequently-repeated phrases ("dreams have
long been a subject of interest and study," "the essay will explore...")
that out-compete more specific, fact-relevant edges in embedding
similarity space. This is a real graph-quality issue (low edge diversity
on this essay, independent of the `bound`-vs-permissive metric question)
and a legitimate target for future work, separate from everything else
in tonight's comparison.
