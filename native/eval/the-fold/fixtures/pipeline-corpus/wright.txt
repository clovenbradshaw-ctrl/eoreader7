Orville and Wilbur Wright were American inventors credited with building and flying the first successful motor-operated airplane. They made the first controlled, sustained flight of a powered aircraft on December 17, 1903, near Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. That first flight lasted 12 seconds and covered 120 feet. The brothers ran a bicycle shop in Dayton, Ohio, before turning to aviation. On the fourth flight of that day, Wilbur Wright flew 852 feet in 59 seconds. The Wright brothers built their first glider in 1900 and flew it at Kitty Hawk because of its steady winds and soft sand. They developed three-axis control of the aircraft, which became the standard for all future airplanes. Their Wright Flyer cost about one thousand dollars to build. Orville Wright survived the first crash of a powered aircraft on September 17, 1908, which killed his passenger, Lieutenant Thomas Selfridge. The Wrights received the patent for their flying machine in the year 1906.
