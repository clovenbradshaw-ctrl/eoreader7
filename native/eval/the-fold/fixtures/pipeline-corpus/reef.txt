The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, located off the coast of Queensland, Australia. It stretches over 2,300 kilometers and is made up of nearly 3,000 individual reefs. The reef is home to thousands of species, including sea turtles, sharks, and over 1,500 species of fish. Rising ocean temperatures have caused repeated mass coral bleaching events in recent years. The reef system covers an area of about 344,000 square kilometers. James Cook charted the outer edge of the reef in the year 1770, when his ship ran aground on it. The Great Barrier Reef was declared a Marine Park in 1975 and a World Heritage Site in 1981. More than ninety percent of the reef has suffered some degree of bleaching since 1998. Tourism brings about two billion dollars a year to the Australian economy from the reef. The crown-of-thorns starfish has destroyed large patches of living coral across the reef's central section.
