# The moral helix — 27 cells of moral development, walked as a spiral

*Written 2026-09-13, companion to THE-MORAL-CORE.md (the ethos as the
license the reason generator composes under) and THE-27-CELLS.md (the cube
the moves land on). THE-MORAL-CORE.md asked how the charters can govern
composition without being toggled off; this document asks the developmental
half of the same question — what a reasoner, human or otherwise, must be
able to COMPOSE before the charter's affordances can govern it, and in what
order those capacities arrive. Standing: **nomination** — a theory,
checkable against the empirical record it cites, which wins any
disagreement in this document. Where a claim here names a mechanism that
exists in the engine, it says so; where it names an empirical finding, it
names the giver; where it proposes, it says that. Amendments append.*

## The one-sentence thesis

**Moral development is the ascent of the cube's 27 cells, walked as a
helix: every moral act is one ring of Ground → Figure → Pattern (the seed's
own cycle), and each ring's residue is the next ring's ground, so the
person who repeatedly composes moral figures compiles a ground that needs
no figure-level work and the act moves up the cube.** A cell is the type of
a moral ACT, never the type of a person — the same refusal the cube already
makes for passages (95.7% of cell assignments survived shuffling the words
inside 2,527 paragraphs; a person is a worse subject than a paragraph, not
a better one). The helix has a height (the highest order of moral move
composable), a width (how many of the 27 are populated), and a pitch (the
cadence at which Pattern re-grounds). The height is measured, never
asserted, and the instruments that measure it are the empirical
developmental ladders — which is where the integral and metamodern projects
pointed and stopped asserting, and where this document stays.

## The tripartite structure — the cube read as moral theory

The space is **27 cells = 9 operators × 3 grains**, never 9×9×9 (THE-27-
CELLS.md §1). Three axes, and each is a real empirical claim before it is a
design choice:

**GRAINS — Ground · Figure · Pattern — the developmental micro-loop.** This
is the helix's pitch. The seed: a ground is a nothing constructed by
perturbing what is present; a figure is a difference from its ground; a
pattern is the difference the figure MADE to the next ground. In moral
terms, one act runs the whole arc — the situation as it stands (Ground),
one judgment cut out of it (Figure), the residue that re-conditions the
next situation (Pattern). The empirical record runs the same arc in three
independent places:

- **Latané & Darley's five steps** (bystander intervention, 1970): notice →
  interpret → assume responsibility → decide → act. Steps 1–2 are Ground;
  3–4 are Figure; 5 is Pattern.
- **Rest's four components** (1983/86): sensitivity (Ground), judgment
  (Figure), motivation and implementation (Pattern).
- **Kohlberg's stages** describe the maturation of the Figure's binding —
  what the judgment anchors to — which presupposes the Ground (sensitivity)
  and deposits the Pattern (character). The stages are the Figure turn seen
  from inside.

*The wheel names the three grains once (`native/docs/THE-WHEEL.md`,
canonical): Ground is the **Void** (the hub), Figure is the **Beings** (the
spokes — "a being is the fold at a point"), Pattern is the **Fold** (the
rim) — "from nothing, the many; from the many, the one." The helix is the
wheel's ring, walked; this document cites the naming, never re-derives it.*

**MODES — Differentiate · Relate · Generate — Rest's components, mapped.**
Differentiate is the cut: noticing that there IS a moral dimension (Rest's
Component 1, moral sensitivity; Haidt's intuition firing first, 2001).
Relate is the put-beside: weighing, comparing, taking perspectives (Rest's
Component 2, moral judgment; Kohlberg's role-taking). Generate is the
bring-into-being: choosing the moral value and carrying it out (Rest's
Components 3–4, motivation and character). Rest's own empirical finding —
that the four components are not one trait and develop and operate
semi-independently — is precisely why the cube's three axes are the right
instrument and a single stage-ladder is the wrong one.

**DOMAINS — Existence · Structure · Interpretation — the registers of the
moral object.** What the act operates on: Existence is the moral substance
— the self, the victim, the harm, what exists (the deontic objects).
Structure is the moral web — relationships, institutions, contracts,
society, how things hang together. Interpretation is the moral holding —
conscience, the frame, the worldview through which the other two are read.
This is the empirical content of what the integral project called "the
quadrants" (interior/exterior × individual/collective) stated as
move-registers rather than as a claim that everyone is right. A norm has
three faces and all three are real: it exists (Existence), it hangs in a
web (Structure), it is held (Interpretation).

**The redundancy is the point.** Terrain and stance both carry grain — a
moral act lands on a place and is performed in a posture, and the two
claims can disagree, which is what makes a moral address falsifiable. The
redundancy is inherited whole from CUBE.md; nothing here loosens it.

## The rules it inherits — EO compliance, stated once

- **Cells classify moves, never content** (P58; FOLD II.12; THE-27-CELLS
  §1). The helix classifies moral ACTS, never persons, never cultures,
  never "stages of a soul." This is the exact inversion of Spiral Dynamics'
  content-memes-as-levels, and it is load-bearing: the empirical record
  supports "a person uses postconventional reasoning at a rate," never "a
  person is postconventional."
- **A prior is a gift and must name its giver** (eo-constitution II.2;
  FOLD II.1). Every empirical anchor below names its giver. A finding
  without one is not admitted.
- **A threshold is earned from a null, never hand-picked** (eo-constitution
  II.10/II.11/II.23; FOLD II.4/II.5/II.11; P19/P70). Stage boundaries in the
  helix are frequency claims measured against a declared baseline (the
  DIT's P-score is already such a frequency), never hand-set cut-points.
- **Amendments append** (eo-constitution IV; FOLD VI; POLICIES header).
- **An empty cell is a lead, never a verdict** (C4/P64). The Ground turn is
  the least-built row of the actual engine AND the most load-bearing for
  morality — that is a lead, not a verdict, on both sides.
- **Coherence is not correspondence** (THE-MORAL-CORE's wall; FOLD II.10).
  The helix orders capacity. A capacity can be norm-consistent and wrong
  about the world. The helix is a floor, never a ceiling.
- **The model is the mouth, not the mind** (FOLD II.9; P2). Assessment of
  moral development is mechanical where possible (DIT scoring is), trained-
  interview where necessary (the SOI), and never a model's self-report.
- **Unwired is failing** (FOLD VI.3; the growth rule). A cell is claimed
  only when its capacity is measured. The helix's own cells are no
  exception: each entry names the instrument that would establish it.

## The helix — three rings, twenty-seven cells

One full pass through the grains is a ring; the pattern of a ring seeds the
ground of the next, so the ring recurs at rising altitude. The three turns
below are one ring shown at three grains; the cells within a turn run in
the cube's operator order (Existence, Structure, Interpretation). For each
cell: **Anchor** names the empirical giver; **Arrest** names the way a
person or system is stuck there; **Helix** names what the cell deposits
into the next.

### Turn 1 — GROUND · the sensitivity ring

*The moral field: constructing, naming, tending, and re-aiming the ground
against which any moral figure can appear. The empirical spine of this turn
is Latané & Darley's first two steps, Rest's Component 1, Haidt's
intuition-first model, and Turiel/Smetana's finding that the moral
dimension is discriminated earlier than any stage theory predicted. It is
also the turn the empirical record says is lost first (ethical fading) and
the turn the engine itself has least built.*

**NUL·Ground — Void · Clearing — *"there is a moral situation here."***
The clearing of the moral ground itself: registering that the ambient
situation is morally relevant at all. Latané & Darley's step one; Rest's
Component 1 (moral sensitivity); Haidt's social intuitionism (2001) — the
first cut is a fast judgment, not a deliberation. The engine's own
cell is `measure` (a declared statistic against a deliberately broken
null): the moral form is the same shape — declare that the situation has a
normative shape at all, tested against the null that it might not.
*Arrest:* the situation passes unperceived — the Darley & Latané (1968)
finding that helping collapses under group size is in large part this
cell's failure, not the judgment's. *Helix:* the door. No figure without
it.

**SIG·Ground — Void · Tending — *"who is here?"***
Naming the moral field's inhabitants: the others present, the victims, the
agents, the witnesses — the field as a population, not a blur. The engine's
`settle` (what would settle the absence) and `cast` (names resolve to WHO,
P11). *Anchor:* Selman's perspective-taking levels (1980) — the field is
populated in stages, from self-only to the differentiated other to Mead's
generalized other; the identifiable-victim effect (Small & Loewenstein 2003;
Kogut & Ritov 2005); compassion collapse as N grows (Västfjäll et al.
2014). *Arrest:* anonymization — Milgram's obedience drops as the learner
is brought into the room, and its inverse, the diffusion of responsibility;
a moral field whose inhabitants are tokens is a void with no tending.
*Helix:* the victim must be a referent, never a surface string (P11); the
moment she becomes a string, cruelty becomes available.

**INS·Ground — Void · Cultivating — *"the field can be other than it is."***
Generating moral ground where none exists: counterfactual moral
imagination — the "what if the roles were reversed" operation, the golden
rule made generative. *Anchor:* norm theory (Kahneman & Miller 1986) — the
counterfactual IS the norm; counterfactual thinking is load-bearing in
moral judgment (Byrne 2005); Rest's sensitivity includes imagining
alternative courses. This is the engine's EMPTY cell (`preflight` — P23's
candidate): the engine cannot yet generate ground where none exists, and
the empirical record says the same is rare in people. *Arrest:* "the way
things are is the way they must be" — the status-quo bias (Samuelson &
Zeckhauser 1988) and the naturalistic fallacy made perceptual. *Helix:*
this is the counterfactual arm the postconventional cells (DEF·Pattern,
REC·Pattern) later load on.

**SEG·Ground — Field · Clearing — *"what does this situation cover?"***
The extent of the moral field, and its units: whose actions fall inside it,
where it ends. *Anchor:* the scope of justice and moral exclusion (Opotow
1990); the moral circle, psychometrically measured (Crimston et al. 2016);
Darley's "social organization of evil" (1992) — exclusion as the mechanism
of institutional harm. *Arrest:* boundary-blindness — the engine's own
year-grain extent that read Hamlin (1861–1865) as complete and could not
see Johnson's six weeks is the same defect as a moral field whose declared
extent cannot see the victim inside it. Moral exclusion is a SEG·Ground
failure: the extent is declared wrong and the figure inside it becomes
invisible. *Helix:* expansion is real but not automatic (the collapse-of-
compassion data) — the extent must be declared, measured, and re-declared;
never defaulted.

**CON·Ground — Field · Tending — *"the field is being maintained."***
The ongoing maintenance of the moral field: keeping the running account
across time without deciding it — the anti-fading discipline. *Anchor:*
ethical fading (Tenbrunsel & Messick 2004) — the slow erosion of the moral
dimension from a decision context as it becomes routine; Bandura's moral
disengagement mechanisms (1999) — eight of them, all erosion of the
maintained field. *Arrest:* the stale field — the routine that stopped
being tended and no one noticed; this is the engine's other EMPTY cell
(`field`, the maintain-the-ground act). Moral development's most fragile
cell: the empirical record says fading is ordinary, not pathological.
*Helix:* this cell is what the summary-refresh gate protects — a moral field
is carried only while its maintenance is real, never by declaration.

**SYN·Ground — Field · Cultivating — *"what has been learned is
sedimented."***
Compiling the accumulated moral field into one carried ground: the residue
of kept fields merged into the self-structure. *Anchor:* chronic
accessibility (Narvaez & Lapsley 2009) — moral functioning becomes automatic
through repeated activation; Rest's schema consolidation — DIT schema
frequencies are stable and consolidate with development; moral identity
internalization (Aquino & Reed 2002). This is Loevinger's ego in one cell:
the WUSCT measures exactly the compiled self-structure. *Arrest:* the
compiled ground that cannot be perturbed — sclerosis, the oracle's disease;
moral rigidity, the person whose field is fully sedimented and closed.
*Helix:* keeping is the boundary (SEED.md). The compiled ground is what the
next ring perceives through; a closed compilation is a closed person.

**DEF·Ground — Atmosphere · Clearing — *"the frame is named."***
Declaring the interpretive moral frame: the ambient normative frame within
which this situation reads. *Anchor:* framing is load-bearing in moral
judgment — Tversky & Kahneman (1981); the trolley/footbridge asymmetry
(Greene 2001) is in large part a frame difference; the omission bias
(Baron & Ritov 1994). The moral foundations (Haidt's MFT) are pre-
structured frames of the field, early-emerging and cross-cultural (Graham
et al. 2011). *Arrest:* frame-blindness — the inability to see the frame as
a choice; the engine's DEF·Ground is the one empty cell whose absence has
never produced a live incident, and the moral form of that is false:
frame-blindness is among the commonest moral failures (the bystander's
pluralistic-ignorance frame is a frame problem). *Helix:* the frame is the
grain at which the whole ring is read; naming it is the difference between
a moral question and a mere preference.

**EVA·Ground — Atmosphere · Tending — *"the frame is being metered."***
Continuously metering the moral frame: checking that the ground is still
being held, without judging a figure. *Anchor:* the honesty-priming
studies — self-awareness reduces cheating (Diener & Wallbom 1976; Mazar,
Amir & Ariely 2008 — the mirror effect); chronic moral self-monitoring is
the empirical mechanism that prevents misconduct. *Arrest:* metering
becomes scorekeeping — the moral ledger as identity (self-righteousness);
or the meter goes stale (the engine's EVA·Ground `atmosphere` has a measured
stale-topic failure). *Helix:* never a gate, never a score — the warmth
you check for (SEED.md). This cell is where THE-MORAL-CORE's PERTURBATION
family lives: measure whether the output still changes when the core is
removed; a stream that no longer changes is a regression of enmeshment.

**REC·Ground — Atmosphere · Cultivating — *"the frame is re-zeroed."***
Re-zeroing the moral frame when it has drifted beyond tolerance: the regime
change, the altitude event. *Anchor:* Kohlberg's cognitive conflict — the
disequilibrium that drives stage transition; the empirical engine of moral
education is exposure to genuinely harder structure — dilemma discussion
plus peer transactive discussion (Berkowitz & Gibbs 1983), effective in the
meta-analysis (Schlaefli, Rest & Thoma 1985). The moral conversion / crisis-
and-reset appears in every moral exemplar's biography (Colby & Damon 1992).
*Arrest:* the frame never re-zeros (moral rigidity — the closed ground) or
re-zeros constantly (moral volatility — no ground ever forms). *Helix:*
this is the lift. The ring ascends here or it does not ascend at all.

### Turn 2 — FIGURE · the judgment ring

*The moral figure: cutting, binding, corroborating, and judging the single
act. The empirical spine is Kohlberg's stages 1–4 (what the judgment binds
to), Rest's three schemas (personal interest → maintaining norms →
postconventional), Turiel's criteria, Milgram's obedience gradients, and
Greene's dual-process finding. This is the turn the classics studied; it is
also the turn the judgment–action gap makes least load-bearing on its own.*

**NUL·Figure — Entity · Dissecting — *"this act is a moral object."***
Cutting one act out of the field as a moral object: establishing that this
particular figure exists and is established — the is-this-wrong decision.
*Anchor:* the Kohlbergian judgment is exactly this — a figure against a
ground; Turiel's domain criterion — children judge harm as wrong even when
the rule is removed (1983), the moral figure survives the removal of the
convention. *Arrest:* reading a moral harm as mere custom (domain
conflation) or moralizing a mere preference (Rozin's moralization of taste,
1997). *Helix:* a figure is a placement against a nothing, or it is refused
(P19) — an act is admitted as morally real only when it clears a moral
ground, never because someone says so.

**SIG·Figure — Entity · Binding — *"who did it, to whom?"***
Resolving the moral actors: agent, victim, witness — referent identity in
the moral field. *Anchor:* attribution and blame (Shaver 1985 — the causal
chain to blameworthiness); dehumanization as the denial of moral status to
out-groups (Haslam 2006) — the empirical precondition of cruelty; Milgram's
proximity gradient again, the victim's individuation as the variable.
*Arrest:* anonymization — the "they" that hides the victim; the referent
collapsed to a token. *Helix:* names resolve to WHO, not to bytes (P11) —
in the moral field, the victim must be a referent; the moment she is a
string, the cruelty becomes an administration problem.

**INS·Figure — Entity · Making — *"a moral act is born."***
Generating the moral act itself: the decision-to-act becoming an act in the
world. *Anchor:* the judgment–action gap — Blasi's (1980) meta-analysis put
the reasoning–action correlation near .3; the gap is the field's founding
problem (Hartshorne & May 1928); implementation intentions (Gollwitzer
1999) are the empirical bridge; moral identity (Blasi 1984) is what closes
the gap in those who cross it. *Arrest:* the schlemiel — knowing and not
doing; the bystander who judges and does not intervene. *Helix:* a moral
resolution that is never born as an act is a wish, not a figure (P14) — the
birth is where a figure becomes something that exists.

**SEG·Figure — Link · Dissecting — *"one piece is cut."***
Cutting the moral figure apart at its seams: which feature of the act is
the moral feature? *Anchor:* Turiel's defining-features program — intrinsic
consequences vs. authority-dependence is the seam; Greene's personal/
impersonal distinction (2001) is the same cut ("who is touched directly");
Knobe's side-effect effect (2003) shows the cut is biased, not neutral.
Psychopathy's specific deficit is the failure to cut moral figures at all
(Blair 1995). *Arrest:* over-cutting (scrupulosity — everything is equally
moral) or under-cutting (nothing is distinguishable). *Helix:* the cut is
derived off the act, never off a label — people say "I'm being fair" while
doing something else; the cut must be taken from the act's own structure
(the engine's `patch`: both small models said INS while supplying a
replacement).

**CON·Figure — Link · Binding — *"this act harms this person."***
Binding the figure to the field: the moral judgment proper — a claim about
the relation between act and victim. *Anchor:* the classic Kohlbergian
judgment; the empirical stages 1–4 are precisely the development of WHAT
the judgment binds to — authority (1), exchange (2), relationship (3),
system (4); Rest's three schemas are three kinds of binding. The harm
foundation is the most cross-culturally endorsed (Haidt's MFT data).
*Arrest:* binding to the wrong anchor — the "just following orders" binding
(Milgram); the authority foundation hijacking the judgment. *Helix:* the
judgment must be span-verified against the act's own bytes (P56) — a moral
judgment that cannot point at the act it binds is a mood, not a judgment.

**SYN·Figure — Link · Making — *"this is corroborated."***
Folding the re-sighting into the same moral note: when the same act recurs,
the moral figure is corroborated, not re-invented; witnesses unioned.
*Anchor:* the character-consistency debate resolved — Hartshorne & May's low
cross-situational consistency was real and is real; consistency appears
when the construct and the situation are both specified, and its honest
form is the density distribution (Fleeson 2001); moral exemplars show
reliability — the SAME moral act across situations (Colby & Damon), which
is corroboration in behavior. *Arrest:* the self-exception — "I was not
myself" — which prevents the pattern from forming (Bandura's
displacement/blame mechanisms). *Helix:* two pages agreeing become one note
with two witnesses, never two notes (P57). Moral character is the
hyperlexicon of one's own acts, and it is built by corroboration, never by
profession.

**DEF·Figure — Lens · Dissecting — *"this figure against this lens is
X."***
Checking a specific moral claim against a declared normative standard: the
cut. *Anchor:* the Defining Issues Test IS this operation — the respondent
weighs specific issue-statements against the dilemma (Rest et al. 1999);
the neo-Kohlbergian finding is that judgment is a weighing of schemas, not
a deduction from a rule. The single cut in the void loop is cardinality
(P53); in the moral field the single cut is harm or right — the figure is
admitted only when it binds to a harm or a right (Turiel's intrinsic-
consequence criterion). *Arrest:* lens-blindness — the "I just feel it's
wrong" that Haidt's moral-dumbfounding studies document (judgment produced,
lens absent, 2000). *Helix:* the lens matures from concrete exchange
("do unto others" as literal trade) to formal abstraction (Rawls's original
position as a figure-cut against a lens) — and the formal lens is a late,
rare acquisition (Rest's P-schema peaks in educated adults).

**EVA·Figure — Lens · Binding — *"the figure is judged."***
Judging the figure through the lens: binding the specific act to the
specific standard and reading the verdict — bound, contradicted, unbound.
*Anchor:* postconventional reasoning (Rest's P-schema) is exactly binding
the act to "moral criteria beyond the existing social order" — it survives
the removal of the authority, the law, the group (Turiel's "would this be
wrong if there were no law against it?"); the DIT's longitudinal evidence
shows the frequency shift PI → MN → P over a decade (Rest, Narvaez, Bebeau
& Thoma 1999). *Arrest:* verdicts that cannot be revised — the certainty
that precludes re-judgment; a check that cannot fail is not a check (II.10;
G1). *Helix:* the verdict is never phrased stronger than its witnesses
("stated by N of M pages") — a moral verdict is never stronger than the
acts that ground it.

**REC·Figure — Lens · Making — *"this figure concedes the lens."***
When a specific judgment fails against a specific standard, the lens is
conceded and the next figure is born: a specific moral error corrected
without re-zeroing the whole frame. *Anchor:* accommodation (Piaget) —
corrective feedback improves moral judgment (the DIT-based education
studies); the empirical apology-and-repair literature — reparative acts
develop and are evaluated (Darby & Schlenker 1982). *Arrest:* never
conceding a single judgment (the grudge — the paradigm defended by refusing
to yield any figure) or conceding everything (spinelessness — every judgment
folds). *Helix:* a later evaluate that disagrees with an earlier determined
verdict concedes it first (P36) — the conscience is an append-only ledger
that concedes; it never rewrites.

### Turn 3 — PATTERN · the character ring

*The moral residue: kinds, networks, principles, standing, and the re-
creation of the paradigm. The empirical spine is Blasi's moral identity,
Colby & Damon's exemplars, chronic accessibility, Rest's postconventional
schema, King & Kitchener's reflective judgment, and Gibbs's argument (2003)
that stages 5–6 are a real but later "existential" phase, not childhood
stages. This is the turn where the ring's residue becomes the next ground —
and where the helix's altitude actually lives.*

**NUL·Pattern — Kind · Unraveling — *"this is a kind of act."***
Challenging and confirming moral kinds: when the same moral act recurs, it
is a type, and the type is tested against the ground. *Anchor:* the moral
foundations are kinds of concern discovered from cross-cultural content
(Haidt's MFT); moral character as a stable kind is real in the density-
distribution sense (Fleeson 2001) and as internalized identity (Aquino &
Reed 2002). *Arrest:* kind-mistakes — one act generalized into a kind
(stereotyping; Bandura's labeling) or kinds refused (each act sui generis —
no moral learning). *Helix:* a kind is declared, never typed in (the
address test, II.12) — "who said this was a kind?" is the question to put
to every moral category, including one's own; the person's own recurring
acts, measured, are the only honest source of their moral kinds.

**SIG·Pattern — Kind · Tracing — *"a pattern is traced."***
Tracing the moral pattern of the field: the recurring actors and their
kinds, signed provisionally from the field's own structure. *Anchor:*
reputation as a moral mechanism — indirect reciprocity (Nowak & Sigmund);
person perception — and its bias, the actor-observer asymmetry (Jones &
Nisbett 1971): we over-trace kinds in others, under-trace them in
ourselves. *Arrest:* over-tracing (the attribution error — condemning the
person, not the act) or under-tracing (the relativism that treats everyone
as opaque). *Helix:* kinds are signed PROVISIONALLY, basin-with-its-own-
null (the engine's `kinds`) — nothing is promoted by induction alone; a
character judgment is a provisional basin-membership, never a verdict on
the person.

**INS·Pattern — Kind · Composing — *"a known procedure is instantiated."***
Composing the moral act from a known moral kind: running the practiced
response onto new material — virtue as skill. *Anchor:* moral expertise as
acquired automaticity (Narvaez 2006); the psychometrically validated
character strengths (Peterson & Seligman 2004); implementation intentions
as the mechanism of virtuous habit (Gollwitzer 1999); Bebeau's dental-
ethics program — professional moral expertise is measurable and trainable
(1994). *Arrest:* the skilled response without a check — the automated
habit that ethical fading exploits; a skill without a check is refused as a
wish (P14). *Helix:* the expert's virtue IS the zero-call response — the
triage nurse does not deliberate each case — but the anchors must be
declared. Automation is only as moral as the declared anchors.

**SEG·Pattern — Network · Unraveling — *"the pattern is cut at its
seams."***
Cutting a moral network apart at its seams: separating what hangs together
from what doesn't — unraveling a conspiracy, a corrupt institution, a false
consensus. *Anchor:* the banality of evil (Arendt 1963) and its empirical
descendants — the finding that the moral NETWORK (roles, authority
gradients, diffusion) is what the individual judgment is bound into
(Milgram's gradient is a network property); whistleblowing research — the
conditions under which the network gets cut and their costs (Miceli &
Near). *Arrest:* the seamless network — "it's the system" as the refusal to
cut; the person who cannot separate the moral from the institutional.
*Helix:* the cut is at the structural fact, never an invented threshold —
the engine's `unravel` refuses a 2-edge-connected network rather than cut
with an invented threshold. Moral courage is cutting the network at its
real seams, parameter-free.

**CON·Pattern — Network · Tracing — *"a recurring arrangement is
bound."***
Binding a recurring moral arrangement: recognizing a practice, a custom, an
institution and binding it as a norm. *Anchor:* Turiel's second domain —
conventions are judged by consensus- and authority-dependence, not by
intrinsic harm; the developmental task is to bind them correctly; Rest's
Maintaining Norms schema is the developmental peak of most adults — the
Conventional stage proper (Kohlberg 4); Durkheim's conscience collective,
whose modern empirical shadow is norm-maintenance. *Arrest:* the arrangement
bound as sacred (the naturalistic fallacy — "it has always been this way")
or unbound into anomie (Durkheim's normlessness). *Helix:* a cycle of one
shape binds nothing — CON relates (the engine's `network`): one instance of
a custom is not a norm; and an unrecognized line is a hole, never a
wildcard.

**SYN·Pattern — Network · Composing — *"relations are linked."***
Composing moral relations into a network: linking judgments to their
neighbors and their cast — the web of obligations. *Anchor:* the care
orientation in its empirical form (Gilligan 1982; Skoe's Ethic of Care
Interview, 1993) — a network of responsibilities, not rules; the empirical
settlement that care and justice are two compositions, not sex-linked
stages (Walker 1984); role obligations and their relational ground (Bowlby's
internal working models as the deepest form). *Arrest:* the unlinked
relation (the isolated judgment — I did right by this person and the web is
not composed) or the network that absorbs the figure (care without justice;
justice without care). *Helix:* relations only make sense linked (the
engine's `graph`) — every moral judgment gets its network shape (↑ before /
↓ after / ⇢ shared cast); the obligation is traced, never asserted.

**DEF·Pattern — Paradigm · Unraveling — *"the declared is challenged."***
Challenging a declared moral principle: testing whether the paradigm's own
general rules hold across the material — the postconventional move.
*Anchor:* Rest's P-schema — moral criteria beyond the existing order; King &
Kitchener's Reflective Judgment (1994), stages 5–7 — the epistemology that
licenses questioning the paradigm; Colby & Damon's exemplars show calm
certainty about principles AND flexibility in application — the principle
held, the application adapted. *Anchor of the ceiling:* Snarey (1985) —
stages 1–4 are cross-culturally robust, 5–6 rare everywhere. *Arrest:* the

## What refutes this

The walls, stated so they are not mistaken for settled:

1. **A person is never a cell.** The same measurement that refuted the cube
   as a content classifier refutes the helix as a person classifier, worse:
   shuffling words leaves 95.7% of cell assignments unchanged; shuffling a
   life's acts is not a licensed perturbation. The helix classifies moves.
   Any use that types a person is a category error by this document's own
   law.
2. **Coherence is not correspondence** (THE-MORAL-CORE's own wall). The
   helix orders capacity. A reasoner with a fully populated Pattern ring
   can be norm-consistent and systematically wrong about the world. The
   helix is a floor, never a ceiling, and a claim that it makes anyone
   moral is the over-claim this document refuses.
3. **The frequencies are the finding, and the frequencies are modest.**
   Rest's own data: the P-schema rises with education and age into
   educated adulthood and plateaus; most adults peak at Maintaining Norms;
   postconventional frequencies are rare cross-culturally (Snarey). The
   helix's upper cells describe a minority capacity; a document that
   implied the ascent is ordinary would be false.
4. **The situationist challenge is real.** The trait tradition is
   contested by the situationist finding (Mischel 1968; Doris 2002) and
   the helix answers it with density and corroboration, not with traits —
   but the challenge is genuine and the Figure ring is where it bites. The
   helix does not resolve the person–situation debate; it inherits it.
5. **Cross-cultural limits.** The upper cells are Western-documented and
   rare; the MFT shows content-pluralism the helix must respect. The helix
   is over moves, and moves are populated with the moral field's OWN
   content — which means the moral circle (SEG·Ground) and the foundations
   (DEF·Ground) carry the cultural load. A helix that assumed its content
   is universal would be the naturalistic fallacy in a grid.
6. **Assessment honesty.** The model is the mouth, not the mind (II.9): no
   model-generated self-report of moral development counts. DIT scoring is
   mechanical; the SOI and RIJ are trained-interview instruments. A cell
   whose instrument is not named is unmeasured, and `unmeasured` is a
   standing, never a pass.
