# Where we are, and where we're going — the spoken version

*A listening version of WHERE-WE-ARE.md, written the third of September,
2026. No tables, no code, no symbols — the numbers are said out loud. The
written version carries the same substance with its citations intact; where
the two differ, the written one and the code win.*

---

## What's actually being built

A reader whose entire state is one append-only record of dated, witnessed,
revisable claims. And whose language model is the mouth of that record —
never its memory, and never its judge.

That last part isn't a slogan. It's a constraint, and it has cost real
capability over and over, and paid for itself every time.

The rule is this. The model may point at a candidate that was gathered
mechanically. It may not write content that becomes ground. It may not
decide what is true.

Every place that line got crossed for convenience, a measurement later found
the crossing had manufactured something. A citation that pointed at a URL
that didn't resolve. A witness that signed its own echo. A caption meaning
"a machine computed this, not a language model" that a small model learned
to imitate one turn later. And the clearest one: asked about the weather
with nothing attached, the system drafted "seventy degrees and sunny" out of
nowhere, and then went and searched the web for it — as though the model's
own invention were the question.

So the wager is that a reading assembled slowly, mechanically, against
controls built to kill it, eventually beats a reading assembled fast and
trusted.

That wager is not yet won. Most of what follows is an honest account of how
far it is from being won — and of the several places where the measurements
say the remaining distance belongs to the material, not to the instrument.

---

## Six ladders

The word "level" was carrying at least four different meanings in
conversation until two of them got split apart and written down. There are
six axes worth tracking, and they're genuinely independent. A project can be
high on one and floored on another, and confusing them is exactly how a pass
ends up tuning the wrong stage.

Here they are, briefly, and then one at a time.

Floors: what grade of thing a mechanism works on. We're at the sixth floor,
and the fifth is the bottleneck.

Strata: which channel of evidence a decision is allowed to rest on. The
first three are solid. The fourth is the entire open question.

Breadth: how many kinds of act the instrument can perform. Twenty-seven out
of twenty-seven. Finished.

Depth: how complex a task it can handle. Stage thirteen on rich material.
Stage eleven in live interaction.

Stations: the pipeline itself, from raw bytes through to the answer. All ten
built and running. One of them is starving all the others.

And ways of knowing: the epistemic surface. Nine of them, all present,
arranged around a hub that is deliberately empty.

### Floors

Floors are about the grade of thing you're operating on. Units, then
referents, then arrangements, then claims, then nested claims, then
corroborated notes, and finally premises.

Floors zero through four are done — done to the material's own ceiling, and
that phrase is carrying real weight. Three separate attempts were made to
push past the roughly twenty percent rate at which a proposition in
narrative prose anchors to a real referent. The bound held every single
time, because it turns out to be a fact about what novels assert, not about
how well we read them.

The floor between claims and notes is nesting — putting a whole claim into
one end of another claim, so that "Tolstoy states that such-and-such" is an
ordinary note. That floor has a wall, and the wall is the reason it's worth
having.

The wall is: witnesses of an outer note never corroborate the inner claim.

Two sources agreeing that Tolstoy says something corroborate that Tolstoy
says it. They give the thing itself nothing. Direct corroboration and
attributed corroboration are kept strictly apart, with no option anywhere in
the code to add them together. And a deliberately planted leak — crediting
an attribution's witness to the inner claim — fails the test that checks for
exactly that.

Without that wall, nesting manufactures corroboration by restating
attribution. Which is precisely how "papers report he said it" turns into
"sources confirm it" out in the world.

### Strata

A stratum is the channel a decision is licensed to rest on. There are four.

Bytes: offsets, spans, addresses. That belongs to the file.

Script: capitalization, punctuation, layout, spelling. Only a reader has
those.

Heard: the word stream in order, closed classes of function words,
recurrence. A listener has those.

And meaning: reference, predication, paraphrase. That takes an understander.

What makes this a ladder rather than just a list is a rule that came from
the user directly: the system must be able to work equally well if it only
heard the novel and didn't read it. Script evidence is allowed to
accelerate. But an organ whose actual decision rests on script alone is
below the bar, and has to say so.

Bytes are disciplined throughout — every span verifies against its own
bytes, coordinate spaces are declared and never silently mixed. Two thousand
five hundred and eighty-four edges out of two thousand five hundred and
eighty-four, self-verified.

The heard layer has been the productive frontier. The clearest example: how
does a listener know that "general" is a title and "Kutuzov" is a name? Not
by rarity — Kutuzov appears five hundred and twenty-nine times in War and
Peace, and "general" six hundred and fifty-seven. A protagonist is not rare.
Not by a hand-written list of titles either; that's a sample of English
standing in for the whole of it.

The answer that worked was to discover the kinds from the word stream
itself. Words get grouped by what tends to come immediately before them, and
each group is named by its own signature rather than by a human label. One
group is "the words that follow the word 'the'" — general, count, emperor,
colonel, captain. Another is "the words that follow nothing, that open their
phrase" — Kutuzov, Napoleon, Pierre, Rostov, Denisov. Nothing was taught.
And a shuffle control dissolves every one of those groups when the company
is destroyed, which is what licenses believing them.

Meaning is where all the remaining difficulty lives. Reference, predication
and paraphrase are the three things no mechanical organ in either repository
can decide, and they're the one place the model is genuinely load-bearing.
More on that shortly, because it turns out to be the crux of everything.

### Breadth

The algebra behind this project names twenty-seven kinds of act — nine
operators crossed with three grains. Every one of those twenty-seven now has
a registered, executable organ. All nine stances are full. All three grains
and all three modes are complete. No operator sits at zero.

Two qualifications, and they matter.

First: an empty cell was always a lead, never a verdict. Of the eighteen
holes that existed originally, more than half turned out to be bookkeeping
debt — real, tested organs that had simply never been registered anywhere.
One was genuine incapacity, and it earned that reading properly, by a
falsifiable prediction written down before the file existed and then
confirmed on real material. And four were errors in the probe itself.

Second, and this is the important one: coherence is strictly weaker than
correspondence. A completed map means every kind of act is performable. It
says absolutely nothing about whether any of them is performed correctly. A
reader can be internally coherent, productive, and systematically
misreading, and no amount of map completion will detect that. Only an oracle
can, and only about facts.

The confirmation that's worth more than the number itself: an independent
census read roughly two hundred and twenty modules the other way round —
module first instead of cell first — and found a second, independently
discovered organ for every one of the twenty-seven cells. Including the two
that previously had exactly one example. Unrelated organs, across text,
audio and MIDI, keep landing on the same cells for unrelated reasons. That's
evidence the algebra isn't just a curated fit to a couple dozen hand-picked
examples.

### Depth

Depth is scored against a received scale of hierarchical complexity.

On War and Peace, the instrument reaches stage thirteen. On an encyclopedic
article about the battle of Borodino, stage six.

That gap is a performance difference, not a failure of the scale — and that
distinction was itself a correction. The first version of the check reported
every difference between materials as a violation, which is simply wrong.
The scale's claim is that a task's complexity doesn't depend on what it's
about. It was never a claim that a performer succeeds equally across
domains.

Borodino's ceiling is real and it's named. The order that fails is pronoun
binding, and Borodino's prose is dominated by collectives — armies, corps,
the French, the Russians — so there's never a clear enough winner. On the
Russian-language version of the same article, that order fails with zero
pronouns even attempted, because the pronoun mechanism runs on an
English-only word list. That's an absent mechanism, not a weak one, and it's
recorded as unbuilt rather than papered over.

There's a second battery that scores interaction rather than reading — can
the system conduct an act, read what came back, and compute its next act
from the result; can it predict an effect before causing it. That reaches
stage eleven against three genuinely different counterparts: the system's
own act grammar over the engine, a real Python subprocess, and a real shell
subprocess. And crucially, no order changed its order-hood depending on
which counterpart it faced.

### Stations

All ten stations of the pipeline are built and running — bytes arriving,
container stripped, extents claimed, the cast discovered, arrangements
extracted, the ledger, kinds, judgment, the turn, and the mouth.

The document that tracks them station by station is now out of date in four
rows, which is worth saying plainly, because a stale map is worse than no
map. The obligation ledger exists now. The kind-standing organ has its
caller — and on real bytes it correctly vetoes folding "Castle Dracula" into
"Count Dracula". The speaker boundary is built; on the real text of Dracula
it finds a hundred and ten sections whose speaker list reads like the
novel's own table of contents. And the subject-verb-object renaming is
finished.

What remains true, and it's the single most important sentence here: station
five starves everything downstream.

## The one number

Here's what station five does. The ledger hears a note the first time, and
folds a later re-sighting into it, combining the witnesses. A note with two
independent witnesses is corroborated. That is the entire theory of knowing
something in this machine.

Measured live, on a real book, on ordinary CPU, with all the walls switched
on: zero point zero one seven clean votes per ask.

One corroborated note per sixty questions asked. On an encyclopedic page
pair it's about double that — still one in thirty. Zero lies, in both runs,
against four deliberately planted fabrications.

And the figure that has been quoted for weeks — that about two percent of
notes reach two witnesses — is an overstatement. The counter was comparing
witness strings with their chunk addresses still attached, so two chunks of
one book counted as two separate sources. It was found by the first walk
over a real novel, where every supposedly corroborated note turned out to
have been corroborated by its own source. That's fixed and pinned now, and
every earlier number computed that way is a record of what the old
instrument said, not something this project stands behind.

But here's the thing. This is not a weak witness. It is the material,
measured.

A novel does not restate its propositions in other chapters. It re-mentions
its referents. The walk found the one thing a diary genuinely says twice —
that the case of Renfield grows more interesting the more you understand the
man — and it honestly reported that everything else a chapter asserts, it
asserts once. Cross-source corroboration is the wrong question to ask of a
single book, and the mechanism answered it without manufacturing a yes.

The design consequence has already landed. The requirement that a note have
two witnesses before it could reach the model is gone. A single-witness note
now reaches the model on its own relevance to the question, with its
corroboration disclosed rather than withheld — the model is told either
"stated in more than one place" or "stated once so far." That gate was
chosen back when the admission door was letting junk through. The door
doesn't anymore, and the gate was starving the model of an entire book's
reading in order to guard against a diet that no longer exists.

## The refutations

The negative results are the real asset here. Four of them reshaped the plan
more than any success did. Each one is written down specifically so nobody
spends another pass retrying it.

**One. Identity is refuted as the corroboration lever.**

Folding two notes together by matching their referents and their word stems
yields exactly zero joins on real pages — within a book and across
documents alike. The flagship example fails by name: "withdraws" and
"retreated" do not share a stem. That is not a morphology gap that can be
closed. It's synonymy, and morphology doesn't touch synonymy.

**Two. Witnessing a bridge works, and cannot reach the wall it was hoped to
reach.**

A bridge is the assumption that two documents are talking about the same
person or place. Asking a model directly about one of those does
discriminate: eight of twelve real correspondences accepted, against two of
twelve on a deliberately mispaired control. Fisher exact test, one-sided,
probability zero point zero one eight, and identical across two runs.

And then the deflating part. All twelve of the candidates examined had two
faces that were the identical string. Prince and Prince. Austria and
Austria. A bridge only exists where an exact triple match has already fired.
So a paraphrase never becomes a candidate, and no witness is ever asked
about it.

The general form of that lesson is worth carrying: an organ that reads a
correspondence can only ever examine correspondences that something else
already proposed.

**Three. No candidate slicer earns a license.**

At face value the numbers looked good — one slicing method separated from
its control nine to one. Then somebody read the nine actual landings instead
of counting them. Page furniture. "Support the Museum." "Visit the Apollo
Journals Website." A video caption. Applying a relatedness check afterward,
nine-to-one becomes one-to-zero. There's essentially no signal left to
license anything with.

And underneath that, a structural finding. The licensing rule names a
particular non-learned check as the guard that bounds the model's authority
on that slot. That check is not below the relevant path at all. And it can't
simply be switched on, because it works by requiring the claim's own literal
words to appear — and the whole point of the mechanism it would guard is to
reach cases where those words are absent. They're mutually exclusive by
design.

**Four. Distributional company is dead as a test of act identity.**

The idea was that two verbs used in similar surroundings mean similar
things. Measured: "saw" and "wrote" score zero point seven four four.
"Looked" and "gazed" — an actual synonym pair — score zero point five eight
five. Company at one word either side measures syntactic frame, not act. The
control caught it before it shipped.

Against those four, one large positive. On a hundred and fifty-eight real
entities and twenty-eight real succession edges, the composition circuit
derived two hundred and twenty-four facts that no source ever stated. Two
hundred and twenty-three of them true. Zero false. Judged by an oracle the
derivation never reads. And zero out of fifty shuffled controls matched it.

Mechanical composition over checked premises works, and works at scale, when
the premises exist. The scarcity of premises is the problem. The composition
is not.

## Where this goes

The order of work hasn't changed and shouldn't be re-derived: ends first,
then identity, then corroboration, then the ledger's mouth, then chat
memory. Fixing note identity before the ends are real would just corroborate
noise.

The mouth is open now. So the live constraint is the two links before it.

And the last week narrowed the identity question considerably. "Are these
two notes the same proposition?" turns out to be a conjunction of three
separate claims, and they have completely different standings.

Do the two ends resolve to the same referents? Largely mechanical today —
there's a referent index within a document, bridges across documents, and
Wikidata identifiers as a received prior.

Does the polarity agree? Yes, mechanical, via a received closed class of
negation words with its source named.

Do the two labels denote the same act? No. That's the whole wall, and
there's no giver for it anywhere in the system.

Two of three are solved. The residue is one question: verb synonymy.

And notice what that zero-join result actually tested. It tested ends and
label together, and its own diagnosis blamed the label half. Which means the
ends half has never been measured on its own. A proposer that requires only
ends correspondence, and deliberately drops the label requirement, is not a
re-run of a refuted experiment. It splits the conjunction at exactly the
joint the postmortem named. It's offline, it costs zero model calls, and it
takes minutes.

### The model was standing in for a giver we didn't have

Here's the reframe that matters.

The place the model is genuinely load-bearing turns out to be one single
question wearing three different costumes. Do these differently-worded
things say the same thing? — asked of two notes, that's identity. Asked of a
claim and a passage, that's the witness. Asked of a claim and a cited
document, that's the primary-source chase.

And that question does not want a model. It wants a giver.

"These two labels denote the same act" is a pattern-grain claim. And the
grain theorem is explicit about those: a corpus can refute one, and can
never earn one. No amount of further reading produces it. Which is exactly
why every distributional attempt has failed, and will keep failing.

This is also the honest answer to "can't we just use Wikipedia?" Yes — but
as a received prior with a named giver, routed through the rule the system
already has, which is that only a given affordance with a named giver
licenses composition. Never as evidence a corpus earns for itself.

Wikidata identifiers and redirects already serve the ends half, and they're
the strongest thing available. Wiktionary's synonym sets are the only layer
that speaks to the actual wall — and they're thesaurus-grade. Loose,
unranked, over-generous. So they'd need their own licensing run, judged on
what they newly admit rather than on how much they cover.

There is a second route that isn't refuted: two labels that repeatedly join
the same pair of resolved ends across independent sources. That has a fatal
structural problem worth naming before anyone spends a pass on it blind. It
is starved by the very wall it would break. A label pair recurring on one
end pair across two independent sources is exactly as rare as corroboration
itself — one in sixty. The distributional route needs the scarcity solved
before it can solve the scarcity.

A received prior has no such problem. It arrives whole, and its size doesn't
depend on how well the reading is going.

The apparatus for receiving that answer landed two days ago. The ledger now
carries the full interpretive triad. Declaring a sameness requires a giver
and a purpose, and lands as a wish — not yet believed. Evaluating it
requires a ground and a declared way of breaking it, and either promotes it
to testimony or refuses it. Conceding it requires the trigger quoted
verbatim, and a conceded commitment makes the notes it was withholding
readable again — which is the property a filter simply cannot have. A
commitment never counts as a witness. And the control that shuffles
everything runs on a scratch log, so an experiment never lands on the
record.

A Wiktionary revision is a giver you can name, and concede when it's wrong.
A model is neither.

### Three things that would change the shape

First, and highest leverage: feed the referent index the system has already
earned into the extraction step. Right now the ends of arrangements anchor
on fragments — only about seven and a half percent are anchored to real
referents, and debris still reaches the ledger, things like "of Moscow"
standing in for a subject. The extractor never consults the cast. Everything
above that wire inherits the improvement.

Second: point the witness at footnote-bound partial notes, with the window
chosen by referent activation rather than by word matching. Of four hundred
and four notes with a readable cited source, two hundred and twenty-six are
partial, and a hundred and sixty-two of those are missing the object — the
source says it in other words, at the right sentence, already located by the
footnote. Word containment sits at parity with its control at every grain,
measured twice. It finds where a note's words live. It does not find the
claim.

Third: finish the split between engine and surface. Roughly seventy modules
still sit in the interaction repository that aren't interaction. The rule
learned from moving the last one: move a closure together, never a single
file.

## What would break this

An honest account owes its own failure conditions.

The stated gate on the whole plan is this: if the memory floor can't raise
clean votes per ask measurably, then the floor's design gets re-examined,
not its tuning. That gate has already fired once, and the re-examination
landed — the two-witness gate on the mouth is gone. The number itself still
hasn't moved. If the giver route also fails to move it, the conclusion is
not "try a tenth lever." It's that cross-source corroboration is the wrong
currency for reading a single work, and the ledger needs a different theory
of when a note may be trusted at all.

The standing wall is the one that no amount of internal success crosses.
Twenty-seven cells, nine full stances, every wall holding, every control
failing where it should. All of that establishes that the instrument is
internally consistent and productive. It does not establish that anything it
reads is true. Only an oracle does, and only about facts. And the two planes
must never share machinery, or the instrument starts proving its own
cognition correct.

And there's a prediction that could break the theory layer. The reading of
the algebra as three mathematics claims the nine stances are one triad
instantiated three times over — which predicts that a stance proven in one
domain transfers to another. That has exactly one earned point from
transfer, and one from deriving a new cell out of its neighbours before
building it. Two points is not a law. A stance that provably does not
transfer would break it, and the document says so itself.

## The short version

Breadth is finished. An independent census found a second organ for all
twenty-seven cells. Depth is high — no scale violations across three
materials, including one that isn't in the Latin alphabet. The floors are
built through premises. The strata are disciplined through what a listener
could hear.

Everything now hangs on one number: one corroborated note per sixty asks.
And on the single question that number reduces to.

Two of that question's three parts are already mechanical. The third is a
pattern-grain claim, which means no corpus will ever earn it, which means
the answer is a named giver and a revisable commitment — not a cleverer
statistic.

The apparatus for receiving that answer is built. What isn't built yet is
the proposer that hands it the candidates. And the first free measurement
that would tell us whether the candidates exist at all.
