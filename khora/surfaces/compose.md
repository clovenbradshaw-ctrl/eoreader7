# The surfaces compose — they call each other, they never re-implement

The folds are many (the holograph, the fold, the chat, the TUI, the council),
and they are one surface at the seam: every one of them presents **Ground@1**,
the same addressed, witnessed record. The rule that keeps them from going
redundant is delegation by address.

## One request, one entrance

A person's words enter through **one** surface. That surface renders what it
owns, and delegates what it does not — to the surface that does, *by address*,
never by re-implementation:

```
   the person
      │  words
      ▼
   THE CHAT  ──draw──▶ penelope:mouth
      │                 │  draw (the irreducible residue)
      │                 ▼
      │             the bridge (heimdall) executes
      │                 │  ground comes back, addressed, witnessed
      ▼                 ▼
   THE CHAT  ──witness──▶ khora:swarm   (hard meaning is swarmed)
      │                 │
      │  ──run-record──▶ the-holograph  (the run's ledger: [S#] sources)
      ▼                 ▼
   THE CHAT  renders the folded answer — the ground + its scars + verdict
```

The chat does not re-read the corpus (khora does), does not re-record the run
(the holograph does), does not re-judge (the council does), does not re-weave
(penelope does). It renders — and it calls.

## What each surface owns

| surface | owns | delegates |
|---|---|---|
| the-fold | the folded world — beings, relations | run-record → holograph; addressed-bytes → khora |
| the-holograph | the run's ledger (SOURCES/RESPONSE/NOTES) | ground-render → the-fold; record → penelope EOT |
| the-chat | the conversation, the interaction | draw → mouth; witness → swarm; run-record → holograph |
| the-tui | the shell, the operator's hand | render → the-fold; run-record → holograph |
| the-council | the contention of the many, the mode that survives | witness → swarm; record → penelope EOT |

## The redundancy is measured, never assumed

Every surface's rendering is a **swatch** row — what it rendered, from whose
ground, at what bytes. A surface that re-implements a seam shows up in the
economy: the same bytes rendered twice from two surfaces instead of one
delegating to the other. The composition rule is enforced the way the house
enforces everything — on the record, by the falsifier:

> a surface that re-implements a seam another surface already owns, or a
> surface whose presented ground has no Ground@1 address, contradicts this.

## The seam is the same everywhere

Whether the words enter the chat, the TUI, or the council, the material crosses
each seam the same way — as **addressed record, never assertion** (Ground@1).
The surfaces differ in what they present, not in how material moves. That is
what keeps a forest of apps from becoming a tower of redundant machines: one
contract, many dream-surfaces, each delegating by address to the surface that
owns the part.
